01 · The problem

Gel is sold on color. Customers buy it on time.

Most nail marketing shows a shade. But the reason someone books a gel manicure is rarely the shade. It is that they have an interview, a wedding or a trip, and they want their hands to be one thing they don't have to think about. OPI positions its Intelli-Gel system around professional wear of up to three weeks, and the system is described as made for nail professionals. The people with the most influence over whether a client chooses it are the techs.

02 · Why techs, not celebrities

The trusted voice is in the chair, not on a billboard

OPI's own positioning is that it was built in service of nail professionals, and its Intelli-Gel launch language is aimed at salons and their clients. This campaign follows that lead: independent nail techs are the creators, because they recommend the shade, the finish and the rebooking date, and their clients already trust them.

03 · Positioning

Positioning statement

For people with a big moment on the calendar, OPI gel is the manicure planned around the next three weeks. Unlike a color-first campaign, it starts with what you have coming up and hands the choice of shade and finish to the tech who knows your hands.

04 · Content pillars

Three formats every partner can make

The Three-Week PlanA 30-second video where a client names their next three weeks and the tech picks the look. Sends viewers to the planner tool.
Day 1 to Day 21A time-lapse of one manicure from the chair to the end of week three. Shows wear honestly. No claims beyond what is true for that client.
Ask Your TechA carousel of four occasion looks with a short script a client can read out in the chair.

06 · Guardrails

Rules every post follows

  • Disclose clearly. The FTC's influencer guidance says free or discounted products count as a financial relationship, and the disclosure should be hard to miss and in the video itself, not buried in hashtags or behind a "more" link. Plain words like "ad" or "sponsored" are preferred over "collab" or "sp".
  • Only claim what is true for that client. No guarantee of three weeks. OPI describes up to three weeks of professional wear, and real wear varies by hands, prep and lifestyle.
  • No health claims. Techs speak about their craft, not about nail health outcomes.
  • Show real hands. No retouching that changes the color, finish or length of the look being shown.
07 · Measurement

How this would be measured

  • Primary KPI: gel bookings attributed to partner QR codes and planner links, per partner.
  • Secondary KPIs: planner completions, share of clients who rebook inside the three-week window, saves and shares per post, cost per booking by tier.
  • Budget split (illustrative): 45% partner fees and kits, 30% paid amplification of top partner posts, 15% planner and QR materials, 10% education events.
  • Test plan: compare occasion-led videos ("Plan Your Three Weeks") against color-led videos to see which drives more bookings per post.
08 · Risks

What could go wrong

  • Over-promising wear. Mitigation: the brief bans guarantees and uses OPI's own "up to" language.
  • Partner posts feel like ads. Mitigation: the tech picks the look. The brand supplies the idea, not the script.
  • Disclosure slip-ups. Mitigation: a one-page disclosure checklist in every partner kit, and a pre-approval step for the first post from each partner.
