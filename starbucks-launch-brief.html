Starbucks · Holiday Menu 2026 · Product Marketing

Order One New Thing.

A launch brief for a menu where the biggest risk isn't awareness. Everyone knows the holiday menu is coming. The risk is that loyal customers order their usual and never meet what's new.

Spec work, not affiliated with or endorsed by Starbucks

1. The launch problem

The 2026 menu launches Nov. 5 with four new drinks and three new food items alongside returning favorites. Starbucks calls the Peppermint Mocha, introduced in 2002, its most popular holiday beverage in company history. That is the asset and the obstacle.

Positioning statement. For Starbucks Rewards members who already have a holiday ritual, the 2026 holiday menu is the season's reason to add one new thing to the order they already love. Unlike a menu that asks customers to switch, it keeps the favorite and gives them a low-risk way to try something new.

2. Who we're talking to

Three segments, defined by what they do at the counter, not by demographics. These are working hypotheses to validate with Rewards purchase data.

Segment A

The Ritualist

Orders the same returning favorite every holiday season (Peppermint Mocha, Caramel Brulée, Sugar Cookie, Gingerbread Chai).

Job to be done: a safe way to try something new without losing the usual.

Segment B

The Afternoon Seeker

Not a coffee-first customer, or already had coffee. Looks for a second drink later in the day.

Job to be done: something festive that isn't another espresso drink.

Segment C

The Lunch Stopper

Visits for food or a midday meal and may not order a seasonal drink at all.

Job to be done: a holiday moment that works as a real meal.

3. Messaging house

One umbrella message, three pillars. Every proof point comes from Starbucks' own announcement or history, not invented claims.

Keep your favorite. Order one new thing.
New flavor, familiar format
Tiramisu Velvet arrives as a latte or a cold brew, so it fits how people already order.
Proof: Tiramisu Velvet Latte (hot or iced) and Tiramisu Velvet Cold Brew, both with velvet mascarpone cold foam and a cocoa and coffee dusting.
A holiday drink for any hour
Bright and non-espresso for the afternoon, creamy for the cozy moment.
Proof: Apple Pomegranate Refresher with pomegranate-flavored pearls; Snowy Apple made with coconut milk.
The menu is a meal now
Savory options mean the holiday menu isn't only dessert.
Proof: Braised Short Rib Grilled Cheese and Turkey & Stuffing Sandwich (new recipe), alongside the Bearista Cake Pop.

4. Launch tiers

Not every item gets the same budget or the same message. Tiering is how a 10+ item menu avoids a flat, forgettable launch.

TierItemsRolePrimary message
Tier 1 · HeroTiramisu Velvet Latte, Tiramisu Velvet Cold BrewThe new-news story. Gets the most paid and owned media."A new holiday flavor, on a drink you already order."
Tier 2 · ReachApple Pomegranate Refresher, Snowy AppleExpands who the menu is for (non-espresso, afternoon)."Holiday, but make it bright."
Tier 3 · AnchorPeppermint Mocha, Caramel Brulée, Sugar Cookie, Gingerbread Chai; returning and new food; merchDrives baseline traffic and carries the pairing prompt. Low incremental media."Your usual is here. Add one new thing."

5. Barista enablement

Product marketing doesn't stop at the ad. The counter is the last and most persuasive channel. Two 10-second lines baristas can use at the register.

When someone orders their usual"Want to add the new Tiramisu Velvet foam to that, or try it on its own next time?"
When someone orders just a drink"The new short rib grilled cheese is a good match if you're staying a while."

7. Risks and mitigations

Risk

New items get overshadowed

The Peppermint Mocha pulls attention.

Mitigation: pair rather than compete. The campaign keeps the favorite in the message.

Risk

Pairing feels pushy

Customers may tune out an upsell.

Mitigation: frame it as a low-stakes "one new thing", and test the barista line before rolling out.

Risk

Red Cup Day has no date

Creative that depends on it could stall.

Mitigation: hold it as a late-season beat, not a launch dependency.
